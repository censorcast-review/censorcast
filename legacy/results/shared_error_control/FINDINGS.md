On the later Hobbies development block, the shared 29-feature head reduces error-head MSE from 2.3554 to 2.3370 and positive-demand-day MSE from 7.8403 to 7.7650. These are descriptive single-seed differences. Calibration-A MSE changes from 2.7284 to 2.7247, while calibration-B MSE increases from 2.5368 to 2.5772. No common A/B threshold satisfies WAPE <= 0.640129832706689 and row coverage >= 35%. The outcome-informed later-block largest-prefix diagnostic also remains infeasible. Thus the matched feature addition modestly improves later-block MSE but does not recover feasibility.

The comparison of shared 21 and shared 29 features removes the sample-size and training-population confounding from the feature-addition contrast. It does not remove those differences when comparing either shared head with a Hobbies specialist. The earlier two specialist results are retained unchanged; all four heads are reported.

The partial outcome oracle concerns selection information conditional on the existing fixed forecast f. Replacing the error estimate with the realized |Y-f| shows what that noncausal, outcome-informed ranking can attain for this forecast; it does not prove that a learnable conditional error head can attain it. Neither this oracle nor similar positive-day error-head MSE excludes point-model misspecification, irreducible outcome variation, or insufficient covariates. This matched feature experiment holds f fixed and therefore does not test a Hobbies-specific point forecast. Existing aggregate point-baseline comparisons cover the pooled population and do not provide a matched Hobbies-specific point-model intervention; we make no claim that they identify the Hobbies bottleneck.
